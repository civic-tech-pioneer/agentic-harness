Slice: S151 — Stage F factory profiles: who writes a client mod's role profiles and its `access/<identity>`, with whose consent

## Goal

Settle, in the design files only, how a client mod (first: watane's factory mod, one
combined mod with one identity) comes to start sessions with role profiles: who writes
`profile/<name>` and `access/<identity>`, how Alice consents to one revision of one
profile for one mod identity (words she reads in the app), what that consent covers,
how she withdraws it, and how a profile offer is adopted. It decides at the consent
boundary (TECHNICAL 14): only the participant gives a client a profile; a requesting
mod never provisions itself; absent or unreadable entries stay denied.

## Acceptance

1. AC1. Read TECHNICAL 2.3, 9.1 and 9.10: a reviewer can name the one writer of
   `profile/`, `access/` and the profile index, the caller it accepts, and every call
   that writes each key.
2. AC2. Follow AGT-J7 through TECHNICAL 9.10: a client's own calls (`profiles`,
   `check`, `start`, an offer) never write an access entry; an entry is written only
   on the host's granted answer to the app's question, which names the client as
   watane knows it, the profile and its reach.
3. AC3. An entry pins one revision (digest) of one profile for one identity; a changed
   profile is not listed or started for that client until Alice allows it again; a
   session's new work is checked against its own revision (9.7 "Revoking").
4. AC4. Withdrawal: removing an entry refuses new work at once and ends that client's
   running turns under that profile in bounded calls; the "Not protected" line of 14
   about revoked entries is replaced by what now holds.
5. AC5. Offers: an offer names no trusted source (W34); adopting it writes a profile
   and gives no mod access; a changed offer stops new use until adopted again (AGT-F-2).
6. AC6. The watane part (host request, app words) is a Needs row of 15; the stage F
   row of 17 lists the build slices, each sized for one slice; cases of 16 cover each
   rule at the lowest level.
7. AC7. N5 is recorded as decided (a) wherever the contracts still call it open.
