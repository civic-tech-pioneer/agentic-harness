## Goal

Alice answers an ask and its card says "Answered, waiting" (in checked mode "Checking with your
judge") in place of its bar until the kernel's decision entry arrives, so she never clicks twice.
A pointer press on the transcript's scrollbar, or on non-focusable text inside an open ask's card,
no longer resumes the follow: the card stays in view until she moves away (S7 RISK a, 18.6 Place).
A session with two open asks behaves: Needs you, focus and the follow (S7 RISK c). And the dock's
rough edges from S7's user-sim are gone: after the "New activity below" pill, focus lands on the
newest row, not the page body; "Needs you (n)" hides while the card it would go to is in view and
holds focus; the pill no longer covers the last card.

## Acceptance

AC1. Answered, waiting. Allow once, Allow `<reach>` this session, or Deny (the deny form's
     submit) on an open ask replaces its bar at once (same frame as the click, before `answer`
     resolves) with a line "Answered, waiting" (`role="status"`), with no answer buttons. The
     card keeps its header, its Input and its diff exactly as before (TECHNICAL 14: the card shows
     the call that will run). When the decision entry for that ask arrives, the card shows the
     decision as today and the line goes.
AC2. Checked mode. While the turn is `running` with activity `checking` (18.5), the line of an
     answered ask reads "Checking with your judge" instead.
AC3. Not counted. An answered ask with no decision entry yet is not counted by "Needs you (n)",
     is not the target of Needs you, not the status line's subject, and its card is not
     `data-asking` (9.4 rule 4: a pending answer counts as decided). Its row stays mounted until
     the decision arrives (as `holdsAsk` keeps open asks today).
AC4. Refused. If `answer` rejects (the kernel refused it, e.g. `busy`), the bar comes back with
     one short line saying the answer was not taken; a refusal whose field is `ask` (the ask is
     already decided, e.g. after a reload, case 9) keeps "Answered, waiting" instead. The fake
     kernel gains an option to refuse `answer`, for the tests.
AC5. Focus after answering. After a click or Enter on an answer button, focus stays inside that
     card (on the answered line, `tabIndex=-1`), never on another card's answer button: Enter
     pressed twice on Allow once of the first of two open asks allows only the first.
AC6. RISK (a). With focus on Allow once in an open ask's card, a `mousedown` on the transcript's
     scrollbar (the scroller element itself, outside its content) or on non-focusable text inside
     the card (the question text, the header, the Input) does not resume the follow: the stream
     keeps arriving and the card stays where it is in the viewport (within 1 px), the "New
     activity below" pill shows. Focus moving to another focusable element outside the card, the
     pill, or the ask being decided resumes it as 18.6 says. A unit test fires the events in jsdom;
     one slow Chromium script (`test/`) drags the real scrollbar and clicks the question text.
AC7. RISK (c). A fixture with two open asks (a story step or a test fixture in `fake/`): Needs you
     reads "Needs you (2)", goes to the first and puts focus on its Allow once; after answering it,
     "Needs you (1)" goes to the second; the follow stays off while focus is in either card; a
     second ask arriving while focus sits on the first neither moves focus nor scrolls the first
     card (its top stays within 1 px).
AC8. Needs you hides while its card is in view and holds focus: with focus inside the card of the
     first open ask and that card fully in the viewport, "Needs you" is not shown when it is the
     only open ask; with more open asks it shows the count and goes to the next open ask whose
     card does not hold focus.
AC9. Pill focus. After a click on "New activity below", focus is on the newest row of the
     transcript (`tabIndex=-1`, focused with `preventScroll`), not `document.body`, and the view
     is at the end.
AC10. Pill does not cover. While the pill shows, the last row's bottom is not under the pill
     (the scroller's end leaves room for it, or the pill sits outside the scroll area); checked in
     the slow Chromium layout script at 1440 and 760 px.
