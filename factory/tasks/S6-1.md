## Interface

Fixed. These are the rules to write.

**P07, active history (9.5, 9.1).** A compaction item returned by the server replaces every
wire item before it: the loop state keeps `active` (the wire index of the newest compaction item,
or of the summary that a `resume-lost` or `failed {too-large}` restart starts from; 0 before
any). Repair and the request body read and send only the items from `active` on; the trim mark
of the body budget lies at or after `active`. `loop/<id>` keeps `wire-chunks` (the first wire
index of each `wire/<id>/<n>` chunk, as the record's `chunks` does for entries), so a request
reads no chunk before the one holding `active`. Chunks wholly before `active` are deleted in the
step that moves it: they hold nothing a request sends, and the transcript entries keep what the
participant sees. So a step's bytes read do not grow with the session's whole history.

**P08, chunk rewrite (9.1, 9.4).** A transcript chunk holds at most 256 entries and at most
64 KiB, or exactly one entry when that entry is over 64 KiB (an entry is at most 256 KiB, 9.4).
An append that would take the last chunk past 64 KiB starts a new chunk. So appending one entry
rewrites at most 64 KiB plus the entry, whatever the transcript's length (about 34 KiB on
average with 4 KiB entries, against about 512 KiB with 1 MiB chunks). Earlier chunks are never
rewritten. The `chunks` cap of 16 384 (9.3) stays; at most 16 384 starts of 8 bytes (u64) is
128 KiB of the 512 KiB record. A `read` page (2 MiB) then reads at most 33 chunks + 1, still
bounded. The B budget's "bytes per finished entry" (17) is where this is measured.

**P09, stream to session (9.1, 9.3, 9.4).** Keys: `stream/<id>` → `{session, kind}` (`kind`:
`provider`, `request`, `job`, `owner`), written in the call that opens or receives the stream,
deleted on its `ended`; `live/<session>` → the id of the session's live owner stream (written by
`attach`, deleted when it ends). The record gains `streams`: the ids of the session's open
streams, at most 10 (1 provider or 1 request, 8 jobs, 1 owner; 9.5), so the restart pass that
writes `unknown` (9.3) deletes their `stream/` keys and `live/<session>` (streams are memory only,
3). An item call reads `stream/<id>` first. A call that only forwards a delta (no finished entry)
reads `stream/<id>` and `live/<session>` only: no session record, no transcript chunk, no wire
chunk, and writes nothing.

**P10, timers (7, 9.3, 9.5, 9.6, 12).** The kernel holds at most one host timer per session,
`s/<session id>`, due at the session's earliest deadline. The record gains `deadlines`: each
`{what, at}`, `what` one of `ask <ask id>` (9.6, `ask-expiry`), `retry` and `resume` (9.5),
`idle` (9.5, the 10-minute release); at most 64 (one per open ask, plus the three others: a 62nd
open ask with a deadline is denied `timers`). When it fires, the kernel handles every due deadline and sets the timer for
the next one, or cancels it. Live session timers are at most 240 kernel-wide (the host allows 256
per mod, 7; 16 are kept for the kernel's own use); the count is in `slots` (9.1) beside the
stream counts and is set to the live count at restart (timers are kept across restarts, 7). A
session that needs a timer at the cap (and the host's `too-many`, if it ever comes) gets, by
deadline: an ask — denied at once, reason `timers`; a retry — the turn ends
`idle {failed {timers}}`; a usage limit — `paused` with no time (the next `say` resumes it, as
after retries ran out); an idle release — the provider stream is released at once. Nothing
waits for a timer slot. A session whose deadlines are all gone frees its timer.
The checked provider's judge timers (10) are the provider's own 256, at most 8 sessions of
its streams: unchanged. 12 gains `failed {timers}` (kernel turn outcome).

**P11, host queue memory (3, 15).** Section 3 row "Byte budgets": queued items (in flight and
kept by a deferral) count against the host's budget of 512 MiB in all, 128 MiB per origin client
(the consumer's 32 streams × 4 MiB), and 16 MiB per page connection (page queues are lossless).
A `push` that would pass the origin client's or the host's budget fails `full`, as the in-flight
limit does (its producer defers or ends, 3, 9.4). A page connection past its 16 MiB ends; its
streams end `lagging`, and the page reattaches after its last index (9.4) and recovers by
replay. 15 row: `host/TECHNICAL.md` H1: byte budgets for queued items (512 MiB host, 128 MiB per
origin client, 16 MiB per page connection; `full`; a page connection past its budget ends
`lagging`) (3; P11).

**P12, kernel quota (9.1, 9.3).** An owner's bytes (`quota/<owner>`) count every key the owner
causes: session records, `queue/`, entries, wire chunks, loop state, `stream/`, `live/` and its
kept `key/<owner>/<key>` results (kept after a delete, so they stay charged). The kernel also
keeps one account for all owners in `quota` (9.1): bytes and sessions. The kernel's state is at
most 256 MiB (host 7); 32 MiB of it is reserved for control records (session records, turn
states, `slots`, `quota`, `stream/`, `live/` and the entries that record a stop), so owners
together hold at most 224 MiB of the rest. Past an owner's 64 sessions or 64 MiB, or past the
kernel's 224 MiB: new sessions and inputs are refused `quota`, and a running turn ends
`stopped {limit: quota}`, written from the reserve. Only a `delete` frees bytes (kept keys stay).
The 64 MiB per owner stays a kernel setting; with the 224 MiB account it can be set higher
without one owner taking the reserve.

54. (9.5, P07) After a compaction, the next request's `input` starts at the compaction item;
    after a summary restart, at the summary; the wire chunks before `active` are gone; a session

55. (9.1, 9.4, P08) Appending a 4 KiB entry to a transcript of 10 and of 10 000 entries rewrites
    at most 64 KiB + the entry; an entry of 100 KiB has a chunk of its own; earlier chunks are
    never written again; `read` and attach give back every entry once across chunk ends.
56. (9.1, 9.4, P09) An item call carrying only a delta reads `stream/<id>` and `live/<session>`
    and nothing else, and writes nothing (count the reads and commits); after a restart, no
    `stream/` key or `live/` key of a stream from before it is left.
57. (9.3, 9.5, 9.6, 7, P10) One session with an open ask, a retry and an idle release holds one
    host timer; it fires each in time order. With 240 session timers live, a new ask is denied
    `timers`, a retry ends `idle {failed {timers}}`, a usage limit pauses with no time, an idle
    release happens at once; 5 owners × 64 paused sessions never get `too-many`.
58. (3, P11) Producers that together pass an origin client's 128 MiB, or the host's 512 MiB, get
    `full` on `push`; a page connection that stops reading past 16 MiB ends, its streams end

59. (9.3, P12) Kept keys are charged to their owner and stay charged after `delete`; owners
    together stop at 224 MiB with `quota`; a turn running when the account fills ends
    `stopped {limit: quota}` and that entry is written; the reserve is never used by entries of
