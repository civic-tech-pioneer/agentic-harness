## Goal

The transcript store (`kernel/src/transcript.rs`) closes the S11 RISKs: an `unreadable` entry
keeps the index of its place in the transcript, never an `i` the stored line claims; `append`
takes only the kinds 9.4 lists for whole entries and refuses `part` and any other kind; `read`
with `max` 0 is refused. The 3.6 s paging test of S11 moves down the pyramid so the kernel's
default run is back near its S11 start.

## Acceptance

AC1. A stored line that is not a readable entry (bad JSON, unknown kind, `v` > 1), sitting at
     index k, reads (via `read` and `replay`) as `{"v":<its v or 1>,"i":k,"kind":"unreadable",
     "at":<its at, when it is a number>,"raw":<the line as a JSON string>}`, with `i` = k even
     when the line claims `"i": 7` or `"i": 99999`. Paging over it neither repeats nor skips an
     index (a test plants such lines at positions 1, middle, last of a chunk).
AC2. `append` with `kind` one of `message`, `tool`, `ask`, `allow`, `verdict`, `turn`,
     `compaction`, `unknown` stores as today. `kind` `part`, `unreadable`, `""`, `Message` or any
     other string returns `Err(KernelError::Refused(vec![Refusal{field:"kind",backend:"",model:"",
     reason}]))`, writes no state (the state's put count is unchanged) and leaves `Meta` unchanged.
AC3. `read(after, before, 0)` returns `Err(KernelError::Refused([... field "max" ...]))` and reads
     no chunk; `read` with `max` 1..=256 behaves as before; over 256 still caps at 256.
     `read`'s signature becomes `-> Result<Page, KernelError>`.
AC4. The S11 test that pages forward and backward through a 3 MiB entry at four page sizes
     (`ac2_check`, `ac2_paging_through_a_big_entry_progresses`) keeps what it proves (paging
     through a group larger than a page always progresses, both ways, and joins byte for byte)
     with a smaller entry and fewer page sizes; `cargo test` for the transcript tests runs under
     1 s. Report the before and after time of `cargo test --test it transcript`.
AC5. `cargo test` and clippy (as `factory/gate.sh`) pass.
