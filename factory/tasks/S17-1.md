## Interface

Rules to write (fixed):
- **B1 (2.1, 15).** `caller.boot() -> u64`: the host's start number, raised once each time the
  host starts and kept in host state; the same for every call until the next start. A pure read:
  not a request, no grant. (watane need; the kernel cannot tell a restart from its own state.)
- **A1 (9.1).** `active/<n>`, n 0..3: page n of the sessions whose turn is `running`, `waiting` or
  `paused`, each entry `{id, boot}` (`boot`: the boot number of the session's last restart step or
  of the call that added it), at most 256 entries per page, so at most 1 024 sessions; a page is
  at most 24 KiB (a session id is at most 64 bytes). `slots.active` holds the four page counts.
  The record's `active-page` names the page that holds the session while it is in the index.
- **A2 (9.3).** A call whose end state moves a session from `idle`, `unknown`, `resume-lost` (or
  a new session) into `running`, `waiting` or `paused` adds it to the first page with room; a call
  that moves it out removes it from its `active-page`. Each such change reads and writes `slots`
  and one page (at most 24 KiB each way) and the record it already writes. A change among
  `running`, `waiting` and `paused`, or a turn that ends and starts the next queued input in the
  same call, writes no page. `delete` removes the session if listed.
- **A3 (9.3, 2.3, 12).** At 1 024 listed sessions, a `start` or `say` that would add one is
  refused `busy` (its message names the cap); it never waits. One owner holds at most 64 sessions
  (9.3), so 16 owners fill it: an accepted limit, as the slots of 9.5.
- **R1 (9.3, detection).** Every top-level call except an item call on a stream
  (`stream-sink.items`, `ended`, `stream-producer.ready`, `ended`; their streams were opened in
  this boot) reads `slots` and calls `caller.boot()`. When they differ, that call first starts the
  pass: it sets `slots.boot`, zeroes the stream slot counts (9.5; `timers` stays, 7), sets
  `slots.restart` to page 0, and sets the kernel's own timer `restart` (one of the 16 of 7) with
  delay 0. It does no session's step itself unless it reads that session's record (R6).
- **R2 (9.3, the pass).** Each firing of `restart` reads `active/` pages from `slots.restart` on
  (at most the 4 pages, 96 KiB) until it finds an entry whose `boot` is older, does that one
  session's restart step, keeps the cursor and sets `restart` again with delay 0; a firing that
  finds none cancels it. So the pass takes at most `A + 1` calls for `A` listed sessions, each
  reading at most 96 KiB of pages plus one session's step, and lists no keys: no prefix scan.
  With no call after a start, nothing runs until one comes (a client's call or a due timer).
- **R3 (9.3, the step's bound).** A session's restart step reads and writes no more than an
  `interrupt` of that session would, plus its page. A second restart during the pass starts a new
  pass from page 0; entries of any older boot are stepped then.
- **R4 (9.3, 9.5, outcomes).** The restart step of a session: deletes its `stream/` keys and
  `live/<session>` of older boots and drops those ids from `streams` (the 9.4 rule, now here);
  sets the record's `boot` and its page entry's `boot`; then by backend and state:
  - Claude, `running` or `waiting`: each open ask is decided `deny`, `by: kernel`, reason
    `restart`, its deadline dropped; the turn becomes `unknown` (written as a `turn` entry and
    pushed), and the session leaves `active/`. The queue is kept.
  - Claude, `paused`: stays `paused`; its `resume` deadline and timer are kept (7); when it fires
    the turn resumes with `session {resume}` (4.1), as after the idle release (9.5).
  - OpenAI, `running` (loop `requesting`, `retrying` or `ready`): the loop goes to `ready` and
    takes a request slot or waits in the FIFO under this boot (9.5); `retrying` keeps its `retry`
    deadline; in `tools`, idempotent jobs start again under their keys, the others get `aborted`
    (9.5, AGT-F-27). The turn stays `running`.
  - OpenAI, `waiting` or `paused`: unchanged; the asks are the kernel's own and their deadlines
    and timer are kept (7).
  - `idle`, `unknown`, `resume-lost` (not listed; reached only by R6): only the stale keys go; a
    Claude session's provider stream of an older boot counts as released, so its next `say`
    resumes with `session {resume}`, as after the idle release.
- **R5 (9.3, 18.5, Continue).** `say` on an `unknown` session starts a turn that resumes the
  Claude session with `session {resume}` (4.1); if `claude` refuses the resume, the turn becomes
  `resume-lost` as today. When inputs are queued, the `say` input joins the end of the queue and
  the turn starts with the oldest one, so order is kept. The console's Continue is `say` with the
  text `Continue`.
- **R6 (9.3, lazy step).** Any call that reads a session record whose `boot` is older than
  `slots.boot` does that session's restart step first, in the same call (so `attach`, `read`,
  `say`, `answer`, `delete` never act on a stale turn); the pass then finds the entry current and
  skips it. `list` reads no extra record for this: for a record of an older boot it reports the
  turn its restart step will give (`unknown` for a Claude `running` or `waiting`), without
  writing.
