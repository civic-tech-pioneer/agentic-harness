## Goal

Stopping a root stops its children, and a restart leaves no half delegation (AGT-J5 "Alice cancels
the product owner's session while the children are live, and both stop"; AGT-F-38; case 24's
lifecycle half). Built on S134-4's association.

## Acceptance

AC1. `interrupt` of a root with two live children: each child's turn ends `interrupted` (handle
     kept), the root's two calls get `aborted` outputs, the root ends `idle {interrupted}`; the
     children's ends write no second root output; the Stop push of the root comes after the
     children's cleanup (9.7).
AC2. `cancel` and `delete` of the root: each live child is cancelled (handle closed), the child
     records stay (never deleted with the root); the owner can still `read` and `delete` them.
AC3. `cancel` or `delete` of a live child: the root's call resolves `aborted` once and the root goes
     on (follow-up request when its other outputs are in). `interrupt` of a live child resolves it
     `{"err":{"code":"interrupted","session":…}}` (S134-4 AC4, here checked from the interrupt call).
AC4. Restart (R4) with two live children, one `paused` (usage limit with a `resume` deadline):
     the root's `task` calls get `aborted` with its other calls, its turn `unknown`; each child
     takes its own step (running -> `unknown`; paused -> its `resume` deadline dropped and
     cancelled, loop idle, turn `unknown`), in either order; no child or root request is sent by
     the restart; the `child` entries and `parent`/`children` links stay; no output reaches the
     root later, also when the owner then `say`s to a child (R5 on the child does not rejoin the
     old call) or to the root.
AC5. A child of an older boot ending after the restart (a late item of its old stream) writes
     nothing to the root.
AC6. Every case of AC1 to AC5 keeps the one-call bound: no call reads more than the root, its live
     children (at most the live cap) and their streams.
